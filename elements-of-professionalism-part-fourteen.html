The Elements of Professionalism, Part Fourteen

The Devil’s in the Details

Friday, November 13, 2026

Hi Team,

This week’s message comes down to one thing: in property management, you cannot be too detailed. Not ever.

I have been doing this for more than 25 years, and almost every problem I have seen started with a detail someone skipped, assumed, or meant to write down later.

Why details matter so much in our business

We are responsible for other people’s most valuable assets and for the homes our residents live in. When something goes wrong, the first questions are always the same: What happened? When? Who was told? What was done about it? Where is the proof?

If the answers are in our records, we can protect the owner, the resident, and Verandah. If they aren’t, we are left with memory and opinion, and that is not enough. If it isn’t documented, we can’t prove it happened.

Where details make or break the outcome

  • Inspections. Photos of every room, every wall, every appliance, and every existing scratch or stain. A security deposit claim is only as strong as the move-in record behind it. In Florida, a landlord has 30 days after move-out to send a written notice of claim on the deposit, so there is no time to go looking for missing evidence.
  • Work orders. What the problem was, who was sent, what they found, what was done, what it cost, and before and after photos. An owner should be able to read a work order and understand it without calling us.
  • Estimates. Every estimate an owner receives should be broken out line by line: each repair or item, the materials, the labor, and the cost of each. A single total like “Repairs — $2,400” tells the owner nothing. A detailed estimate lets them see exactly what they are paying for, compare it fairly, and approve it with confidence. When an estimate is vague, owners hesitate, ask questions, and sometimes start to wonder whether they are being overcharged.
  • Communication. Every call, text, and email with an owner, resident, or vendor gets noted: the date, who said what, and what was agreed. “I told them” is not a record.
  • Leases and documents. Names spelled correctly, dates, rent amounts, deposits, addenda, and signatures. One wrong number on a lease can follow us for the entire tenancy.
  • Owner statements. Every charge should be clear and explained. When an owner has to ask what something is, we have already lost a little of their trust.

Details are how we take care of each other

Last week I explained that property management is one job shared across our team. Details are what make that work. When you finish your part, the next person should be able to pick it up without guessing, without calling you, and without redoing anything.

A complete note takes a few extra minutes. A missing one can cost a teammate an hour, cost an owner money, or cost us a client.

What “detailed” looks like

Write as if someone who has never seen the file will read it six months from now, because someone often will. That might be a teammate, an owner, an attorney, or a judge.

  • Use full names, dates, and times.
  • Say what happened, not just that something happened.
  • Attach the photo, the invoice, or the email instead of describing it.
  • Write down what happens next and who is responsible for it.
Ask yourself this week
  1. If I were out tomorrow, could a teammate pick up everything I’m working on from my notes alone?
  2. Is there a conversation from this week that I haven’t written down yet?
  3. Would an owner reading my last work order or note understand exactly what happened without asking?

You will never get in trouble with me for being too thorough. Take the extra few minutes. It protects our owners, our residents, your teammates, and you.

Thank you for the care you put into your work.
